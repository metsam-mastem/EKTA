# Collaboration Engineering Atlas

**Record ID:** EKTA-MTH-000001  
**Type:** Method / Engineering Practice Domain  
**Status:** PROPOSED — 0.1.0  
**Scope:** Individual, pair, group, team, business partnership, inter-organizational, community, open-source, and human–AI collaboration.

## 1. Definition and boundary

Collaboration Engineering is the systematic design, operation, measurement, and improvement of how two or more actors coordinate contributions toward shared or interdependent outcomes. It specifies purpose, participants, boundaries, roles, decision rights, interaction protocols, workflows, information exchange, resources, agreements, controls, evidence, and lifecycle.

It is related to, but not synonymous with, project management, people management, organizational design, teamwork, communication, or task tracking. Those disciplines may be composed into a collaboration system.

## 2. Outcomes

A collaboration system should enable participants to align on purpose and scope; identify actors, roles, authority, and accountability; coordinate work and dependencies; make traceable decisions; exchange information and preserve knowledge; manage conflict, risk, change, and resources; verify deliverables; and improve from evidence.

Individual; pair; group; stable or temporary team; cross-functional or cross-team; department; organization-wide; inter-organizational partnership; consortium or alliance; community or network; open-source project; human–AI or multi-agent system.

### Relationship and incentives
Cooperative; competitive; coopetitive; contractual service delivery; strategic alliance; co-creation; co-design; co-development; co-production; open innovation.

These are orthogonal dimensions, not mutually exclusive categories in one flat taxonomy.

## 5. Collaboration lifecycle

1. **Discover:** identify problem, opportunity, stakeholders, and context.
2. **Align:** define mission, outcomes, scope, constraints, and success measures.
3. **Constitute:** establish participants, structure, roles, authority, and membership.
4. **Agree:** adopt working agreements, communication rules, policies, and required contracts.
5. **Plan:** define deliverables, workflow, dependencies, capacity, risks, and acceptance criteria.
6. **Execute:** coordinate work, decisions, reviews, handoffs, and information exchange.
7. **Monitor:** inspect progress, workload, quality, risks, and collaboration health.
8. **Evaluate:** verify outcomes and assess effectiveness, efficiency, fairness, and compliance.
9. **Improve:** update agreements, processes, knowledge, and system design.
10. **Transition or close:** transfer ownership and knowledge, settle obligations, archive records, and revoke access where appropriate.

### Responsibility and decision tools
- **RACI / RASCI:** responsibility assignment; not a complete decision process.
- **DACI:** decision driver, approver, contributors, informed.
- **RAPID:** recommend, agree, perform, input, decide.
- **Delegation of Authority:** formal approval and spending limits.
- **ADR:** traceable record of an architectural decision, not a meeting transcript.

## 10. Team Topologies reference model

Team Topologies describes four fundamental team types: stream-aligned, platform, enabling, and complicated-subsystem. Its three interaction modes are collaboration, X-as-a-Service, and facilitation. Apply it where team boundaries and cognitive load are relevant; do not assume it is a universal organization chart.

## 11. Governance and controls

Define authority; accountability; transparency; participation; conflict handling; change control; records and retention; security; and assurance. Apply the lightest control that adequately manages the risk. Avoid documents and meetings without a decision, control, delivery, or learning purpose.

## 12. Measurement model

Candidate measures include outcome attainment; delivery lead and cycle time; work in progress and blocked time; handoff delay and rework; decision latency and reversals; dependency failure; quality and acceptance; stakeholder satisfaction and team health; cost per accepted outcome; knowledge reuse and onboarding time; risk exposure and control effectiveness.

Every metric needs a definition, unit, population, time window, owner, source, calculation, and limitations. Do not optimize one metric such as utilization or task count in isolation.

## 13. Collaboration maturity model

Proposed EKTA model, not an ISO maturity scale:
- **L0 — Ad hoc:** informal personal coordination.
- **L1 — Defined:** purpose, roles, and basic agreements documented.
- **L2 — Managed:** workflows, decisions, risks, and delivery managed.
- **L3 — Integrated:** cross-team / cross-organization interfaces and governance connected.
- **L4 — Optimized:** evidence-driven improvement and adaptive coordination sustained.

Assess dimensions separately; do not assign a maturity score without evidence and scoring rules.

## 14. Human–AI collaboration extension

Treat an AI agent as a bounded technical actor, not as an unaccountable employee or legal person. Define agent identity, owner, purpose, operating boundary, tool permissions, data scope, input/output contracts, provenance, approval gates, escalation, failure behavior, logs, human accountability and override, revocation, shutdown, and incident response.
