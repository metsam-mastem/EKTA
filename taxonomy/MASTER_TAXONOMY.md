# EKTA Master Taxonomy

EKTA classifies knowledge along three orthogonal axes: engineering domain, engineering layer, and knowledge type. These answer different questions and must not be collapsed into one tree.

## A. Engineering domains
1. Software Engineering
2. Computer Engineering and Computer Architecture
3. Operating Systems and Platform Engineering
4. Data Engineering and Database Systems
5. Networking and Telecommunications
6. Electrical and Electronic Engineering
7. Semiconductor and Integrated Circuit Engineering
8. Embedded Systems and Firmware
9. Mechanical Engineering
10. Materials Science and Manufacturing
11. Systems Engineering and Systems Architecture
12. Control Engineering and Automation
13. Robotics and Mechatronics
14. Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning Systems
15. Cybersecurity and Privacy Engineering
16. Safety, Reliability, and Resilience Engineering
17. Human Factors, UX, UI, and HMI
18. DevOps, SRE, and Operations Engineering
19. Product and Project Engineering
20. Biomedical and Industrial Engineering
21. Energy and Power Systems
22. Aerospace, Automotive, and Transportation Systems
23. Media, Graphics, Simulation, and Game Engineering

## B. Engineering layers
1. Mission, business, and requirements
2. Human, experience, and interaction
3. Application, agent, and domain logic
4. Workflow, orchestration, and process
5. Service, API, and data
6. Platform, framework, and runtime
7. Operating system and system services
8. Firmware, drivers, and embedded control
9. Hardware architecture and computation
10. Circuit, physical, mechanical, and manufacturing

Cross-cutting concerns: security, safety, performance, reliability, observability, interoperability, maintainability, accessibility, cost, sustainability.

## C. Knowledge types
Concept, principle, architecture, component, interface, pattern, anti-pattern, technique, method, algorithm, data structure, process, workflow, standard, specification, tool, framework, platform, metric, model, failure mode, hazard, research, evidence, decision, test, playbook, checklist, implementation, example.

## D. Rules
Multi-tagging is allowed. Runtime is a concept; Workflow Runtime is a scoped specialization. An implementation is not the concept it implements. A tool is not a technique; a standard is not a tool. A workflow is not necessarily an engine. A layer is an architectural position, not a discipline.
